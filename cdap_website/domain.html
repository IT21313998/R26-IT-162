Research Domain & Methodology

Detailed literature survey, identified research gaps, formal research problem, primary objectives, and technological framework.

1. Literature Survey

Flooding along the lower Kelani River basin is driven by intense rainfall in the central highlands and steep runoff characteristics. In Sri Lanka, current flood forecasting systems operated by the Department of Meteorology and the Irrigation Department utilize numerical hydrological models (e.g., HEC-HMS) calibrated on historical catchment data.

However, existing literature confirms two persistent limitations:

  • Sparse Physical Gauging: Rain gauge stations are spaced tens of kilometers apart, failing to capture localized cloudburst events and high-intensity rainfall pockets across sub-catchments.
  • Delayed Dissemination: Bulletins published via television broadcasts and SMS broadcasts lack dynamic spatial resolution, offering coarse provincial warnings rather than street-level actionable warnings.

Recent advances in edge IoT microcontrollers (ESP32) and deep recurrent neural networks (LSTM, Bi-LSTM) offer a breakthrough for real-time hydrological time-series modeling with exogenous atmospheric variables.

2. Research Gap

While satellite-based rainfall estimates and centralized river gauge models exist independently, there is a distinct absence of an integrated, low-cost hyperlocal IoT sensing network paired with real-time multi-horizon machine learning models (24h, 48h, 72h) calibrated specifically for Sri Lankan river basins with automated upstream-downstream data fusion.

3. Research Problem

"How can a distributed network of low-cost IoT rainfall sensors and multi-variable atmospheric telemetry be fused with deep learning regression models to provide accurate, multi-horizon river water level predictions and distance-based dynamic flood risk warnings to vulnerable Kelani Riverine communities?"

4. Research Objectives

General Objective

To design, develop, deploy, and evaluate an intelligent IoT-based flood early warning and water level prediction system that delivers hyperlocal 1-to-3 day ahead forecasts to Kelani riverine communities.

Specific Objectives

  • Develop solar-powered ESP32 nodes measuring rainfall depth (±3% accuracy).
  • Ingest atmospheric variables (pressure tendencies, dew point) via Open-Meteo API.
  • Train and benchmark Bi-LSTM, LightGBM, and Random Forest models achieving >85% accuracy.
  • Formulate Distance-Based Dynamic Flood Risk Scoring (DFRS) and trilingual warnings.

5. Methodology

The research adopts an iterative experimental design methodology spanning 5 phased stages:

Phase 1: Data Collection & Hardware

Designing ESP32 firmware with tipping bucket interrupts, SIM800L GPRS telemetry, and sourcing 3.65 years (31,992 hourly samples) of Kelani climate data.

Phase 2: Feature Engineering & ML

Extracting Magnus dew point depression, vapor pressure deficit, multi-scale rolling lag aggregations (1h to 72h), and cyclical time features.

Phase 3: Integration & Evaluation

Deploying REST APIs (FastAPI) and interactive Streamlit live dashboards with automated fallback mechanisms and trilingual emergency alerting.
